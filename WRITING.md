# Writing for the player

Every word a player sees — prompts, feedback, "How it works", stories, hints — follows this.
The reader is 13–14. Write at a Year 6–8 reading level: a tired kid on a phone should get
it in one read.

Why: simplifying the *language* of maths questions, without touching the maths, raised
scores for 1,174 Year 8 students — most for weaker readers (Abedi & Lord 2001). The changes
that worked are the rules below. Plain-language guides (digital.gov, NN/g, the Australian
Style Manual) say the same: short sentences, everyday words, active voice, lists.

## Rules

1. **One idea per sentence.** Aim for 12 words or fewer; never more than 20.
2. **Everyday words.** "the number in front of x", not "the coefficient". "the same line",
   not "coincident". "true", not "satisfied". "kind of problem", not "schema".
3. **Maths words get explained the first time** in a screen: "gradient (how steep it is)",
   "y-intercept (where it crosses the y-axis)", "the crossing point".
4. **Say "you".** Active voice: "Divide both sides by 3", not "Both sides are divided by 3".
5. **No long chains.** Split "if…, then…, which means…" into two or three short sentences.
6. **Show, don't describe.** "−4y + y = −3y" beats "collecting like terms with different signs".
7. **Feedback = what went wrong, then what to do.** Two short sentences. Then stop.
   - Not quite: "That's the x-axis. The y-intercept is on the y-axis, where x = 0."
   - Right: "Yes! Every term × 3 — the 10 on the right too."
8. **Stories: short and concrete.** Who, what, numbers. One fact per sentence. The question
   on its own line at the end.
9. **Few dashes, no semicolons.** Use a full stop instead.
10. **Encouraging, never babyish.** No "Oopsie", no "simply", no "obviously". Praise the
    move ("You kept the bracket"), not the person.

## Words we use (always the same ones)

| Say | Not |
|-----|-----|
| gradient (how steep) | slope coefficient, m-value |
| y-intercept (crosses the y-axis) | c-value, vertical intercept |
| crossing point | intersection (explain once: "the crossing point, also called the intersection") |
| works in both / makes both true | satisfies both |
| the same line | coincident, equivalent |
| number in front of x | coefficient |
| take away | subtract (both fine — but be consistent within a screen) |
